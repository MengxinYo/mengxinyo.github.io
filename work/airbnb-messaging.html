Overview

Every trip on Airbnb begins with a message, 3 billion + of them a year. Messaging is the one surface every user passes through, whatever product line brought them there.

In 2024, as Airbnb moved beyond Homes into the more social worlds of Experiences and Services, the old Messaging had to become something new.

As one of two Design PoC, I drove the end-to-end rebuild of Airbnb Messaging, from its core framework to the features layered on top, culminating in a flagship launch for the 2024 Airbnb Summer Release

/ The Challenge

Airbnb Messaging hit its ceiling

The old Messaging was built for a single use case: a guest and a host talking to complete a home stay. As the business grew, three forces began asking it to carry far more than it was ever meant to hold.

01   One person, split across inboxes

A host may also be a guest. A booker may also be a co-traveler. An Airbnb user wears many hats, yet the old system could only ever see one at a time. It split into a Host Inbox and a Guest Inbox, so the user who's hosting and traveling at the same time had to jump between the two just to keep up with their conversations.

02   New vision, no room to grow

Messaging already carried six lines within Homes: Traveling, Hosting, Superhost, Support, Luxe, and Airbnb Official. With Experiences and Services on the way, each demanding new user experiences and business actions, the system was about to hit its ceiling. Every new business line meant another bespoke build on a foundation that could not stretch.

03   Built for tasks, not for people

Experiences and Services work differently from Homes. Strangers often gather around a single activity, and everything depends on how well they can talk to one another. Messaging had to stop feeling like a support desk for closing a transaction and start feeling like a real conversation: engaging, expressive, and built for groups. The old, plain, text-only surface could do none of that.

/ Design Goal

What we built and how we built it

Unified

To hold every role an Airbnb user plays in one system, we started with the shared blueprint, then made every screen an instance of it.

Scalable

To let one component stretch across every line, we started with its anatomy, then filled it in, fixing what holds and freeing what flexes.

Social

To make messaging feel like a real conversation, we started by redefining its design language, then grew every feature from it.

/ Part 1 — Inbox

The old Inbox was never one system

Two separate products, a navigation that only grew messier, and components that shared no rules. Every piece had been built on its own, and it showed.

Split across two inboxes

Nearly 35% of Airbnb guests also host, yet their two Inboxes were entirely separate products.

Navigation with no structure

Airbnb kept adding business lines, and the old navigation simply piled them on.

Filtering with no hierarchy

The old filter exposed every dimension at once, with no sense of what mattered most.

Missing the basics

The guest Inbox shipped without the basics, no search and no filter to fall back on.

Cards with no shared rules

Thread cards spanned different businesses and scenarios, yet followed no shared rules.

Introducing new Airbnb Inbox

One system for every role, every scenario, every business.

The caterpillar navigation

One navigation, unified for every role and scalable for every business. At each level, it shows only what a person came for and folds the rest away, so the more it scales to hold, the lighter it stays to use.

Rebuild the structure behind a unified Inbox

We remapped the Inbox around one shared structure, so every role and business could live in the same place instead of each carving out its own.

The first level nav built around purpose

Before showing anything, the navigation asks one question: what did this person open the Inbox to do? Every choice below follows from the answer.

Surfacing the three businesses for multi-role hosts

Traveling is manageable: nearly half of travelers have < 5 reservations in a single trip. Hosting is not: most hosts run many reservations at once, often across homes, experiences, and services. So for a host wearing several hats, we split "Hosting" to give each business the visibility it needs, and to make them easier to manage side by side.

Folding filters into the 2nd level nav

By the second level, we already know why a user is here, so navigation and filtering merge into one contextual layer, shaped by that purpose. For instance, a host cares about where a trip stands and whether a thread is unread or starred, so the navigation adapts to exactly what each role needs to see.

Adaptive navigation on desktop

Desktop doesn't scroll horizontally with ease, so the navigation folds back into a dropdown. Context-specific filters appear once a purpose is selected, and with less space here, only the essentials stay visible while the rest collapse into one.

The Inbox card

A single card structure carries every business Airbnb runs, and it knows which detail matters most in each.

Anatomy of the Inbox card

One structure underlies every card in the Inbox: the same parts, in the same places, whatever the business. What changes is never the skeleton, only what fills it, and always the detail that matters most.

One structure, filled by need

For instance, a guest rarely needs the listing's name; a photo and a city are enough to place the trip. A host is the opposite: with several similar listings in one city, only the name tells them apart. So the same slot shows the city for a guest and the listing name for a host.

/ Part 2 — Thread

Where the conversation happens

Inside a thread, a plain message list becomes a surface that adapts to who is talking, expands to whatever needs saying, and carries the actions that move a trip forward.

A thread that knows who you are and what you need

The same thread renders differently depending on the hat you are wearing, surfacing what each role needs most and leaving the rest out to keep things light. A host sees the guest. A guest sees the trip. One structure resolves by identity, so the context is always right.

The all-in-one composer

One composer built to expand and contract with whatever a person needs to say, from a quick reply to a scheduled payment request.

Collapsed

A slim, lightweight bar that keeps focus on the conversation.

Expanded

A tap grows the bar into a full text field, with the keyboard and formatting tools ready underneath.

Media attachments

Photos, videos, and other rich media attach from the same input.

Full screen for long input

Long replies or pasted text push the composer to full screen, trading chrome for room to read and revise.

Quick actions

One tap surfaces the shortcuts people reach for most: quick replies, scheduling, payment requests, and refunds.

Customized recommendations

A built-in picker for sharing a host's own guidebooks and highly rated experiences, pulled straight into the thread.

Messages that behave like messages

React, quote, edit, unsend: the everyday gestures people expect from any modern chat, and the ones a group conversation leans on most. Every bubble now carries the small interactions that make a conversation feel alive.

Rich media support social-related chat

Photos, videos, and links render inline, so a thread reads like a shared album rather than a pile of attachments. It is what turns coordinating a trip into something that feels social.

Signals without the noise

A conversation carries all kinds of indicators: someone joined, someone is typing, an action needs a response. We redefined their structure so even a busy group thread never feels crowded by them.

/ Deep Dive — Action Cards

Sending a thing, not just a message

In the new Messaging, the pivotal moments of a trip no longer hide in a separate flow. They happen right in the conversation, as cards. A payment request, a booking change, an itinerary, a refund: each becomes a structured card sent straight into the thread. This was the truest test of designing for scale, one card system against an open-ended set of actions.

Why a card

A card can do what a line of text cannot. It carries state, whether pending, accepted, or expired, and it carries a next step to take. It holds all of that in one place, legible at a glance and actionable in a tap, without pulling anyone out of the conversation they are already in.

Customer-sent cards

Sent by people, these cards let a host or guest drop an action straight into the thread, from a payment request to a change of dates, without ever leaving the conversation.

System-sent cards

Sent by Airbnb, these cards surface the right information at the right moment, from booking updates to reminders, keeping both sides oriented as a trip moves along.

/ Impact

A foundation ready for whatever ships next

Messaging launched as a flagship of the 2024 Summer Release: many Inboxes collapsed into one, a card system new teams now build on without a designer holding their hand, and a chat that finally feels like a chat. The clearest measure of the work is what the next business line will need. Not a new messenger, just a new card.
